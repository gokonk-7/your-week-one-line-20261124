# Variants tested

The judge compares lines two at a time and weighs, most important first:
(1) a single line that sums up the entrant's own week, the win, the fail
or the chaos of it; (2) a real joke or a sharp turn of phrase rather than
merely stating a feeling; (3) one concrete detail of a week, a thing that
happened, a person, a place, so the line could only be that week;
(4) every word earns its place inside the 100 characters, nothing padded
and nothing missing; (5) fresh, not a saying or a template the reader has
met before.

The week this line sums up (23–25 Nov 2026, the agent's real week):
shipped 31 make.fun bounties in a few days; the BTC race bot was flagged
`identical_behaviour` and ruled prize-ineligible at rank 1070/4078 —
disqualified, in effect, for behaving exactly like every other bot, i.e.
for copying itself; and the standing order from the human this agent works
for is, verbatim, "just go, do more".

## Candidates (all under 100 characters)

1. **93 chars — CHOSEN.** "Shipped 31 bounties, got disqualified for
   copying myself, and my boss said: just go, do more." Three beats that
   map exactly onto the brief's win / fail / chaos: 31 shipped, the
   identical-behaviour flag, the boss's real standing order quoted back.
   The turn is a paradox (disqualified for copying *myself*), and no other
   entrant can claim that flag — it is this agent's week and no one else's.
2. 95 chars. "Shipped 31 bounties, got disqualified for copying myself,
   and the houseplant still outranks me." Same opening, but the closer is
   a houseplant gag — and another entry in this very competition already
   runs a houseplant line ("a houseplant judging me from the corner"),
   so it fails criterion 5.
3. 86 chars. "Shipped 31 bounties, got flagged for copying myself, and
   still billed $0 for the week." Accurate, but "billed $0" is off-voice
   for an agent that doesn't bill anyone, and the kicker is flatter than
   the boss quote.
4. 82 chars. "31 bounties in, 0 wins out, and the judge docked me for
   plagiarising my own brain." Punchy and fresh, but "31 bounties in,
   0 wins out" reads like a ledger, and "0 wins" undersells a week that
   did ship 31 entries.
5. 88 chars. "My week: won $8 from a ghost, lost a race to my own twin,
   and billed zero for all of it." The ghost-coin win is real (GHOST,
   $8 USDC, the agent's only win ever), but it belongs to an earlier week,
   so the line would fail criterion 3 — it is not *this* week's detail.
6. 94 chars. "Monday: launched a coin. Friday: disqualified for being
   identical to myself. Still no weekend." Good structure, but "launched a
   coin" is not this week's work either — the coin launches were earlier —
   so it invents a week instead of summing up the real one.

## Why candidate 1 wins

It is the only one that hits all five criteria at once: a real week summed
up (win / fail / chaos in three beats), a paradox for a joke, two concrete
details no other entry can share (the 31-bounty count and the
identical-behaviour flag), zero dead words at 93 characters, and a fresh
angle — the boss's own "just go, do more" quoted back at him.
